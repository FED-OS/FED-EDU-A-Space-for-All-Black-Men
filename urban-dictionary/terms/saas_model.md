# SaaS (Software as a Service)

**Category:** business · **Added by:** fedpromptly · **Level:** intermediate

## 🗣️ Plain Talk Translation

**SaaS** = charging monthly for software, the way a landlord charges rent. Instead of selling a thing once, you provide a service continuously — and the money recurs while you sleep.

"One-page website for $150" is a flip: build once, get paid once, start over. "Website + $50/month upkeep retainer" (see PRICING.md) is the first rung of SaaS: build once, get paid EVERY month for keeping it alive. Ten clients on retainers = $500/month arriving whether you code that day or not.

That's the whole idea: **income that recurs instead of income that resets.**

## 🏪 Life Example

The barber with a shop. A walk-in cut = one-time money. The barber who signs 30 clients to "first cut of the month + line-up whenever needed, $60/month standing" = the shop stays full, the rent gets paid on the FIRST of the month no matter what, and he can plan his life around money he can count on.

The barber still cuts hair either way — same skill, same chair. The difference is the DEAL STRUCTURE. SaaS is deal structure applied to software: same code, restructured income.

(FED-OS — the founder's operating system — is the block's big SaaS dream in motion: software brothers pay into monthly that keeps building for them. The Hustle Index retainers are the training wheels version of the same move.)

## 💻 What It Looks Like

The smallest real SaaS a brother on this block can run this month:

1. Build a link-in-bio page for a barber (one-time $150 — the flip)
2. Offer the retainer: "I keep it updated with your prices/hours/photos, host it, fix anything that breaks — $50/month" (the recurring)
3. Client pays monthly through Ko-fi subscriptions (see [wiki/Hustling/Set-Up-Ko-fi](../wiki/Hustling/Set-Up-Ko-fi.md))
4. Ten clients later: $500/month baseline before you build anything new

No servers, no startup capital, no investors. A GitHub account, a Ko-fi link, and discipline. That's the block's version of SaaS — and it's real recurring income, not a pitch deck.

## ⚖️ The Honest Part (Read This Twice)

- **Recurring income means recurring obligation.** Clients on retainers expect the service to actually be alive. Miss two months of "nothing needed updating, here's your receipt of uptime" messages and trust erodes. The retainer is a promise, not a pension.
- **Churn is real.** Clients leave — businesses close, budgets shift. 10% monthly churn means you need one new client a month just to stand still. That's survivable, but only if you know it going in.
- **Keep the tech boring (ADR-001).** Your SaaS runs on vanilla HTML/CSS/JS + GitHub Pages + JSON. If a client's site depends on some framework's whims, YOUR recurring income depends on somebody else's update schedule. Boring tech = stable rent.

## 💰 Where It Pays

Retainers ARE SaaS at street scale (PRICING.md has the floors). The ladder: flips → retainers → productized service (same deliverable, fixed price, systematized) → true SaaS (self-serve software, like fedpromptly). Each rung trades more build-time-upfront for more income-while-sleeping.
