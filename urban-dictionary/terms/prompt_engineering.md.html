Prompt Engineering

Category: ai · Added by: fedpromptly · Level: beginner (the block's home turf)

🗣️ Plain Talk Translation

Prompt engineering is talking to AI the right way. AI does what you ASK, not what you MEAN — and prompt engineering is the skill of closing that gap.

"Draw me" gets you nothing. "Draw a full-color comic panel of a young Black man at a workbench in a dark room, green monitor glow, textbooks stacked beside him, determined face, cinematic lighting" gets you a poster. Same AI. Different asker.

That's the whole skill: specificity is the price of quality.

🏪 Life Example

Every brother has watched somebody walk up to a barber and say "just a line-up" — then walk out mad because "just a line-up" left the barber guessing. The next man says "zero on the sides, scissors on top, taper in the back, line-up following my natural hairline, and I need it to look sharp for Friday." He walks out looking exactly how he pictured. Same barber, same chair, same clippers.

The barber was the AI both times. The difference was the client.

That's prompt engineering. The tool has skill; the asker controls the outcome. (And it's why this org exists — see CITATIONS.md: fedpromptly.com, the founder's AI prompt platform, is literally this skill turned into a product.)

💻 What It Looks Like

The pattern that works (the one every prompt in prompts/ follows):

1. ROLE — who the AI should be
   "You are a patient coding coach for absolute beginners."

2. AUDIENCE — who it's talking to
   "I'm on a cracked phone, first time touching code."

3. RULES — the constraints
   "No jargon without translation. One concept at a time."

4. FORMAT — what the output looks like
   "Analogy first, then the real term, then my next action."

5. CONTEXT — your specific situation
   "Now: explain what a variable is, for a 45-year-old warehouse worker."

Missing any of the five = the barber guessing. All five = the fresh cut.
