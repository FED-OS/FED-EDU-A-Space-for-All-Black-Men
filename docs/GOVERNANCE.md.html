🏛️ FED-EDU GOVERNANCE — How The Block Is Run

This is a community, not a company. But communities that last have structure. Here's how decisions get made, who makes them, and how a brother's voice gets heard.

The Structure

The Founders

The architects who laid the foundation (FED-OS / fedpromptly leadership). They hold the keys to the org, the domain, and the deployment pipeline. Their job is to keep the mission pure — not to rule.

The Maintainers

Brothers listed in MAINTAINERS.md who keep specific wings of the project running. Each module has at least one maintainer responsible for reviewing changes, welcoming new members, and keeping docs current. Maintainers earn their role by shipping — there is no other path to it.

The Members

Every brother in the org. Members propose, build, vote, teach, and level up. Members in good standing (active in the last 90 days) vote on community decisions.

The Elders Council

A rotating group of senior members — respected for wisdom, consistency, and how they treat people. The council mediates disputes, advises on direction, and holds the culture when the community grows fast. Older brothers are especially encouraged to serve. Old dogs teach new tricks.

How Decisions Get Made

Level 1 — Just Do It (daily stuff). Typo fixes, wiki entries, new urban-dictionary terms, guide improvements, helping brothers in the Help Desk. No vote needed. Ship it. If it's wrong, we fix it forward.

Level 2 — Discussion Vote (module-level). New features, changes to a blueprint, new courses, pricing standard changes. Post in the discussions with the [PROPOSAL] tag. 7 days of open comment, then maintainers of the affected module decide with member feedback weighing heavily.

Level 3 — Community Vote (movement-level). Changes to the Manifesto, the ground rules, the license, new sectors of the excellence matrix, partnerships, or spending community funds. A formal discussion with the [VOTE] tag. 14 days open. Members in good standing vote. Simple majority passes; manifesto changes need two-thirds.

Level 4 — Founder Veto (rare). Only for things that would break the mission: selling the community, closing the source, excluding any group of Black men from the space, or anything that puts young brothers at risk. The veto exists to protect the mission, nothing else.

Community Funds

Ko-fi and other contributions are tracked publicly in the support-the-block/ records.

  • Transparency: every dollar in and every dollar out gets logged in a public ledger (donation amounts, dates, what was purchased — personal donor info stays private).
  • Uses: refurbished desktops for raffles, data plans for tethered learners, hosting costs, shipping costs. That's it. No one gets paid a salary from community funds.
  • Approval: purchases over $100 need a Level 3 community vote. Under $100, any two maintainers can approve, logged publicly.

Disputes & Discipline

Step 1 — Talk it out. Most friction dies when brothers communicate directly. Use the DMs, keep it respectful.

Step 2 — Mediation. If it can't be worked out, an Elder mediates privately. Both sides get heard.

Step 3 — The Table. Serious or repeated violations of the Code of Conduct go to the maintainers. Accusations get evidence. The accused gets to respond. Sanctions range from a warning to a temporary cool-off to removal from the org. Gatekeeping, elitism, harassment, endangering minors — instant removal, no vote needed. The rules that protect beginners and kids are non-negotiable.

Step 4 — Appeal. Removed members can appeal to the Elders Council after 30 days. The council's ruling is final.

New Modules

Any brother can propose a new module (Level 2 vote). To be considered, a proposal needs: the problem it solves, who it serves, a starter file structure, and at least one brother willing to maintain it. We'd rather have ten small, well-kept rooms than one giant hallway nobody cleans.

Regional Chapters

As the community grows, regional crews (Jamaica, Haiti, Nigeria, UK, Atlanta, Chicago, London, Toronto...) can organize chapters. Chapters use the same Code of Conduct, fly under the FED-EDU flag, and get listed in community-boards/. Chapters handle their own local events; the global org handles the platform. One flag, many blocks.

The Long Game

Governance exists so the block outlives any individual. Founders step back, maintainers rotate, elders pass the torch. The mission is bigger than all of us: every kind of Black man, learning and building and owning, forever. Everything in this file serves that.
