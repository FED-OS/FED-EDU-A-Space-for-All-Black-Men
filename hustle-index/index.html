A simple approach

Make progress in four small steps.

This is not a competition or a complicated system. It is a gentle way to move from “I should” to “I did.”

1

Choose one outcome

Say what you want in one clear sentence. Example: “I want to publish a one-page site by Friday.”

2

Make it smaller

Find a first action that fits into 30 minutes. A small completed task is more useful than a big unfinished plan.

3

Try it

Do the small version, then notice what happened. You do not need a perfect result to learn something useful.

4

Choose the next step

Keep going, adjust your approach, or stop. Any of those choices can be the right one when you have learned enough.

People helping people

Ask clearly. Share kindly.

A good community does not require special vocabulary. If you want help, say what you are trying to do, what you have already tried, and what kind of answer would help.

❓

Ask a question

Start with your goal. Add a little context. Finish with one specific question that someone can answer.

✅

Share a small win

Finished a first draft or tried a new idea? A short update can help others and keep you moving.

🤝

Help someone else

If you have a useful answer, share it simply. Experience is welcome, but kindness matters more than expertise.

Keep this site useful and honest.

The Block is designed to be free and easy to use. If you add donations, memberships, events, or community tools later, make the purpose, cost, and rules clear before asking anyone to participate.

Common questions

You do not need to know everything before you start.

Is The Block for beginners?

Yes. The site uses plain language and encourages small first steps. You can skip anything that is not useful to you.

Do I need to pay to use the resources?

No. This starter site presents the resources as free. If you later add paid options, explain exactly what people receive and what they will pay.

What should I do first?

Choose one of the three starting points at the top of the page. If you are unsure, use the next-step planner and focus on the action that takes 30 minutes or less.
