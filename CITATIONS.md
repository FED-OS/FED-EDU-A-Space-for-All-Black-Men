# 📖 CITATIONS — Sources, Inspirations & Standing On Shoulders

Every movement has roots. These are ours. The organizations and platforms below either directly inspired FED-EDU's design or are doing the work in the world that this project extends into the digital space. Respect to all of them.

## Direct Inspirations

**Spill** — The Black culture-first social platform built by Alphonzo Terrell and Devaris Brown. Proof that when former Twitter engineers from the culture build their own space instead of being an afterthought in someone else's, it works. Spill is the reason this founder went looking for an equivalent space in tech — and found nothing. So we built it.
- https://www.spill.com

**FED-OS & fedpromptly** — The operating environment and AI engine this project plugs into. The parent ecosystem.
- https://fedpromptly.com

## Organizations Doing The Work

**The Hidden Genius Project** — Trains and mentors Black male youth in technology creation, entrepreneurship, and leadership. The gold standard for holistic, community-focused tech mentorship.
- https://www.hiddengeniusproject.org

**Black in AI** — Global, multi-institutional initiative increasing the presence of Black individuals in AI. Workshops, networking, career advancement.
- https://www.blackinai.org

**BLK Men in Tech** — Closing the representation gap for Black men in the tech workforce and leadership through mentorship, networking, and scholarships.
- https://www.blkmenintech.com

**Black Boys Code** — Introducing young Black men to coding, robotics, and AI fluency; partnered with Morehouse College on advanced programs.
- https://blackboyscode.com

**All Star Code** — Free summer intensives giving young men underrepresented in tech the coding and AI fluency skills they need.
- https://www.allstarcode.org

**blackcomputeHER** — Supporting computing + tech education for the Black community.
- https://blackcomputeher.org

## Historical & Cultural References

The generational arc framing — Kunta Kinte, Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., President Barack Obama — draws on:

- *Roots: The Saga of an American Family* (Alex Haley, 1976) and the 1977 television adaptation — Kunta Kinte's refusal to surrender his name and mind.
- Dr. King's Poor People's Campaign (1968) — the economic justice blueprint; "the right to sit at a lunch counter meant nothing if you couldn't afford the burger."
- The presidency of Barack Obama (2009–2017) — proof of systemic mastery at the highest level.
