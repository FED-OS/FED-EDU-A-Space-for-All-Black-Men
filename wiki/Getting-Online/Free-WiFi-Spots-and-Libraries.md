# 📚 Free WiFi Spots and Libraries — Getting Online When Money's Tight

**Plain terms:** Tethering is the main play, but sometimes you need a fast connection for an hour (big downloads, a video course, uploading a big project). This page maps the free (and safe) places to get online — and how to use public computers without leaving your passwords behind.

## 🗺️ Where the Free Internet Lives

| Spot | What you get | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| **Public library** | Free computers + free WiFi + quiet + PRINTERS | Sessions are time-limited (usually 60-90 min), may need a library card (free) |
| **McDonald's / Starbucks / Chick-fil-A** | Free WiFi, no purchase required at most | Time limits at some locations, 2-hour courtesy norm |
| **Community centers & churches** | Often free WiFi and sometimes free computer labs | Hours vary — call ahead |
| **Some laundromats & barbershops** | Free WiFi while you wait | Ask, don't assume |
| **City WiFi initiatives** | Some cities run free public WiFi downtown | Search "[your city] free public WiFi" |
| **A brother's house** | The original WiFi spot | Bring something, be respectful of the time |
| **School/college campus** | If you're enrolled — or walk the outside edges, the signal often leaks | Enrollment or proximity |

**Pro move:** The [Libby app](https://libbyapp.com) with a library card = free ebooks and audiobooks on tech, straight to your phone. The library is the block's free university and most brothers drive past it every day.

## ⚠️ Public Computer Survival Rules (READ THIS — PROTECT YOURSELF)

Library and school computers are shared machines. Brothers come after you, and brothers went before you. These rules keep everybody safe:

1. **Never save passwords on a public computer.** When the browser asks "Remember this password?" — the answer is **NEVER** on a public machine.
2. **Use a Private/Incognito window.** Chrome: three dots → New Incognito Tab. Firefox: menu → New Private Window. It forgets everything when you close it. This is the ONE habit that saves your accounts.
3. **When you're done: sign out, then close the browser.** Both steps. Signing out kills the session; Incognito forgetting means there's nothing to find.
4. **Watch your screen in public.** Your password shouldn't be visible to the brother next to you. Cover it like a poker hand.
5. **Two-factor (2FA) on your GitHub.** If a public machine ever catches a keylogger, 2FA saves your account. Settings → Password and authentication → Enable 2FA. Do it today — it's the one security move that protects everything else (see SECURITY.md).

## 📱 The Phone-First Backup Plan

No PC at all yet? Everything in this operation is built to work **on a phone alone**:

- **GitHub's mobile site** (github.com in the phone browser) lets you read, edit, and push code — from the phone. Full browser mode, not the app.
- **The PWA move:** Visit the FED-EDU site → browser menu → "Add to Home Screen." Now it sits on your home screen like a real app, works offline for pages you've already visited, and doesn't eat data.
- **Budget move:** The library PC for the heavy work, the phone for daily reading and light edits. That combination carries a brother all the way to his first job.

## 🏁 Your Move

Three-part mission, all free:
1. Find your nearest public library — search "[your city] public library hours" right now.
2. Ask for a library card (free, usually just needs an ID or proof of address — some libraries no longer even require that).
3. While you're there, log into GitHub in an Incognito window and make sure 2FA is on.

You now have a free office, free internet, free books, and free printing. That's a real setup for $0.
